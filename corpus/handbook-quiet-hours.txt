Lost ID/Meal Cards/Keys/Key Card: Students who have lost their ID card/meal card/key card should report it to Residence Life and ﬁll out a lost ID card form. Residence Life will make you a new card for free if it is your ﬁrst time losing it. Additional cards will have an associated cost to the student. For lost room keys, house keys, and suite keys, there will be a replacement charge (and note that all roommates will need new keys but only the student who lost the key will be charged). If a student wants to dine on campus and does not have their ID/meal card, the student must pay the guest meal price (with credit, food service venues are cashless). However, the student will be issued a receipt and can receive a refund, in full, if the student presents the receipt and their valid ID at the Food Service Oﬃce within 72 hours. ID cards are considered a part of your College record. Falsiﬁcation or tampering with College records (ID/meal card/key card) is considered a serious oﬀense. Violators are subject to disciplinary action, a fee, and possible loss of ID/meal card/key card. Lost keys or IDs are to be taken to the Residence Life Oﬃce (if open) or Security and they will try to track down the owner to return it. Lounges and Public Areas: Lounges are provided for the use of residents and their guests but are not to be used for the accommodation of overnight guests. Students are responsible collectively for the condition of lounges and common areas, such as bathrooms and kitchens, in their part of the building. The costs for repairing any damages to these common areas that cannot be assigned to a speciﬁc individual will be shared by all residents of that area of the building. For safety reasons, residents or their guests may not sleep overnight in the lounge areas. Missing Residents: T he College is obligated by law to endeavor to ﬁnd residential students who are missing. If you must leave campus (even for 24 hours), make sure that your RA, or advisor, or other college oﬃcial knows your whereabouts. See the complete policy . Motorized Vehicles: No motorcycles or other motorized vehicles may be brought into the residence halls. Noise/Quiet Hours/Sound Equipment Policy: The residents of all residence halls have the responsibility to be considerate of others at all times (24 hour courtesy hours). This means respecting another’s right to sleep, study and not be disturbed. Each resident has the responsibility to confront any noise problem that is of concern to them and to call upon a RA if assistance is needed. Each ﬂoor will decide on guidelines to help maintain an atmosphere that will respect the rights of those residents who are studying or sleeping. No stereos or sound equipment should be audible outside one’s room. Residence Life reserves the right to conﬁscate sound ampliﬁcation equipment for repeated or ﬂagrant violations of the noise policy. Again, out of consideration for others we require all residents to observe “Quiet Hours.” 68
